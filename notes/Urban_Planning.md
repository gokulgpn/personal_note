- [LinkedIn post by Tirthankar "TC" Chakraborty](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7478690057026207745/): Critiques the common practice of using satellite-derived land surface temperature (LST) maps to communicate heatwave severity. Argues LST — the radiometric "skin" temperature of whatever a satellite sees (rooftops, asphalt, tree canopy tops) — is routinely conflated with air temperature, which is what people actually feel, inflating perceived heat hazard and overstating the cooling benefit of trees on air temperature since shading lowers ground LST far more than it lowers daytime air temperature. Replies debate whether the fix is to stop using LST for this purpose outright or to better communicate its limitations, and link to supporting research on urban-heat-island air-temperature vs. LST discrepancies and overestimation of income-based heat disparities when using LST. Keywords: land surface temperature, urban heat island, heatwaves, remote sensing, air temperature, science communication. Related: [[Remote_Sensing]], [[Climate_Change]]
